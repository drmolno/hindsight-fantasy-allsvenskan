Fantasy Allsvenskan-In Hindsight

This project explores the question of the maximal possible Fantasy Allsvenskan score for the 2026 season. Instead of playing the game in the standard, predictive manner, we look at the season in hindstight, that is, with the knowledge of how all players performed, and try to figure out the play that would have given the highest number of points. We phrase the problem as a mixed-integer linear program (MILP). The sheer size of this program forces certain approximations and clever solutions. On this page we present the best solutions we have found, and invite all nerds to compete.

Introduction

Allsvenskan is the Swedish highest national football league. It includes 16 teams from Malmö in the south, to Uppsala in central Sweden (no northern teams since GIF Sundsvall were relegated in 2022). The league is played over 30 game weeks, from April through November. Mjällby AIF are reigning champions with the all time 75 points record from 2025.

For football enthusaists who did not make the big leagues as players, Fantasy Allsvenskan provides an alternative chance to be the best, or at least to dominate the local friends league. Based on the enourmously popular Fantasy Premier League (fpl: ), Fantasy Allsvenskan is a strategy game where the objective is to compose a squad of Allsvenskan players, who are then scored based on their real life performance. With the option of transfering players between game weeks, the selection of starting line up (11 out of 15 player squad), and the choice of captain (one player who’s score is counted twice), it is a highly complex and stochastic predictive problem.

In this project, we remove stochasticity from the equation, to try to approach the maximal possible score. And we do this by looking at the season in hindsight, i.e., knowing exactly how many points each players scored in each game week. For instance, while predicting 4 goals from Heintz against VSK is unlikely, choosing to play him in hindsight, with the knowledge of these goals, is obvious. Yet, even with 20/20 hindsight vision, the problem is highlly complex.

Looking at the each game week separately, it is feasible (while not trivial as budget and positions have to be considered) to select the best 11 players. However, as more complex rules, such as transfer costs and booster chips are added, the optimization complexity also grows.

Alpscode solved the hindsight optimization problem for FPL the season 2020/2021 by phrasing it as a mixed-integer linear program (MILP), and managed to score …, where the best predictive human player (Joshua Bull: a mathematician) scored 2557 (link to ). This big difference goes to show the difficulty of the predictive problem (define this above). Anyway, in this project, we also approach the problem with trough MILP, with player selections and chip activation as decision variables.
