Fantasy Allsvenskan, in Hindsight

This project explores the maximal possible Fantasy Allsvenskan score for the 2026 season. Instead of playing the game in the standard, predictive manner, we look at the season in hindstight, that is, with the knowledge of how all players performed, and try to figure out the play that would have given the highest number of points. We phrase the problem as a integer program. The sheer size of this program forces certain approximations and clever solutions. On this page we present the best solutions we have found, and invite all nerds to compete.

Introduction

Allsvenskan is the Swedish highest national football league. It includes 16 teams from Malmö in the south, to Uppsala in central Sweden (no northern teams since GIF Sundsvall were relegated in 2022). The league is played over 30 game weeks, from April through November. Mjällby AIF are reigning champions with the all time 75 points record from 2025.

For football enthusaists who did not make the big leagues as players, Fantasy Allsvenskan provides an alternative chance to be the best, or at least to dominate the local friends league. Based on the enourmously popular Fantasy Premier League (fpl: ), Fantasy Allsvenskan is a strategy game where the objective is to compose a squad of Allsvenskan players, who are then scored based on their real life performance. With the option of transfering players between game weeks, the selection of starting line up (11 out of 15 player squad), and the choice of captain (one player who’s score is counted twice), it is a highly complex and stochastic predictive problem.

In this project, we remove stochasticity from the equation, to try to approach the maximal possible score. And we do this by looking at the season in hindsight, i.e., knowing exactly how many points each players scored in each game week. For instance, while predicting 4 goals from Heintz against VSK is unlikely, choosing to play him in hindsight, with the knowledge of these goals, is obvious. Yet, even with 20/20 hindsight vision, the problem is highly complex.

Looking at the each game week separately, it is feasible (while not trivial as budget and positions have to be considered) to select the best 11 players. However, as more complex rules, such as transfer costs and booster chips are added, the optimization complexity also grows.

Rules

Fantasy Allsvenskan is played by selecting a squad of 15 players and then pick the best 11 out of these as starters, and among the starters to select a captain. The squad should consist of 2 goal keepers, 5 defenders, 5 midfielders and 3 attackers, and the starting 11 of 1 goal keeper, 3-5 defenders, 3-5 midfielders, and 1-3 attackers. Furthermore, there can be at most 3 players from any of the Allsvenskan teams.

Points are rewarded to players based on their real life game performance. A goal scored gives 5 points, an assist gives 3 points, and a clean sheet for a keeper gives 5 points, for instance. The exact points awarded are described here (). The captain will get double his amount of points.

The points are counted every week, and players can adjust their teams in between. In the end, the player with the most cumulative points, over all game weeks is the winner.

Pruning

Open problems

Here follows a set of problems to work on for those who are interested.

Score better, or show a lower upper bound

As the best found solution we have is not proven optimal, it might be possible to close the optimality gap. Either by finding a better solution, or a lower upper bound.

Find proofs that certain pruning strategies never reduce the score

It might be possible to prove that certain players are truly useless, i.e., that there are always a strictly better alternative. Pruning such players would then speed up the solution of the problem without sacrifising points.

Find other fun player selections

For instance: best only playing at home, only playing at grass, only using Norwegians, etc, etc.
